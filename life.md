title: Her life

standfirst: Ester Karen Aida, born Karen Aida Morritt, 1967 – 9 August 2022.
---

Ester Karen Aida was a poet and a visual artist in Jerusalem. She painted,
drew, embroidered and wrote, and she worked for peace between Arabs and Jews in
the city she lived in. Michael Dickel, who knew her from poetry readings there,
wrote that she did it with words and deeds both: she helped people who needed
help, arranged dialogue meetings, and encouraged others into Nonviolent
Communication training.

She was born Karen Aida Morritt in 1967 and grew up in Newfield, New York, near
Ithaca. She moved to Israel in 1991 and stayed, seven years of it in Tekoa, in
the hills south-east of Jerusalem, and the rest in Jerusalem itself. She
studied in the Shaindy Rudoff Graduate Program in Creative Writing at Bar-Ilan
University, and she and Jane Medved became the first poetry editors of *The
Ilanot Review*. She was an artist as well, so she designed the covers too, back
when the journal was printed on paper. Ester was a name she took for herself
later, after her heart transplant. She signed her poems and her art Ester Karen
Aida, her own names and the surname she was born to.

Jane Medved wrote that her poems draw on Jewish wisdom and on her faith in the
Creator of the Universe, and that they follow the soul through unlikely
friendships and the tests of love. She argues with God in them. *Is it any
different with you, God?* she asks in “In the Hart Museum.” And she keeps
noticing the people around her: the baby shrieking on the bus, the shopkeepers
in Bethlehem turning off their Christmas lights, the friend she wants to go and
buy an embroidered dress with.

She was part of the poetry life of the city, at gallery readings and Jerusalism
events and among the people she had met in the writing program, and that is
where many of her friendships were. Dickel first met her at a reading in a
gallery, organized by their mutual friend Lonnie Monka. She was in a
wheelchair, he wrote, but active, engaged and cheerful. Over the years he knew
her she went from wheelchair to walker to cane. She wrote and showed her work
through fourteen years of physical struggle and intervention, without letting
that become the whole of her subject. Her poems and essays appeared in *The
BeZine* and on Dickel’s blog *Meta/Phor(e)/Play*, with her paintings, drawings
and needlework alongside them.

In 2017 she spent nine days in Beit Jala at an international training in
Nonviolent Communication, and wrote about it afterwards in
[Empathy Cafe](https://thebezine.com/portfolio/empathy-cafe-ester-karen-aida/),
a letter to an American friend. It is the clearest account she left of what she
was trying to do: a religious Jew who had lived in a settlement, sitting in a
room of Palestinians and Israelis, trying to speak for her own people without
speaking against anyone else’s.

She described herself, in a note she kept with her poems, simply:

<blockquote class="acknowledgements">
<p>Ester Karen Aida received the gift of a heart transplant in Jerusalem from a
Palestinian mom in 2009. She has arranged dialogue meetings for the Dati-Leumi
sector in coordination with NVC – Bethlehem. A mother of six, she lives in
Jerusalem.</p>
</blockquote>

On 9 August 2022, during what should have been a simple catheterization
procedure, the borrowed heart gave out. She was buried in Jerusalem that
evening.
