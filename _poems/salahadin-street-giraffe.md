A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
They look down at the tricolored strata of their big coffees.
Someone thinks, wtf no alcohol here
We need a Palestinian at this table, says a woman of the cloth.
We try to keep a low profile,
They explain at East Jerusalem Baptist,
their campus, peanut butter in the cracks
between Dormition Abbey and Shulchan David Wedding Hall.

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
Actually, the Educational Bookshop at Salah a Din.

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
Wait, there are two Jews. I mean three.
I mean two priests. And me?
My teacher did Vipassana. Who am I?

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar
So different from the vast seascapes of Brasil,
in my preconception. Let’s not teach anthropology but be it,
Tell kin, Aye, kin, have you ever arrived a friend of the bride
and returned, related to the groom? Cohen, ken.

How many priests does it take to change
the flickering light?

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar
because God loves your heart like white wild iris
and glory be if you grow among the rocks of the field.
Funny, you don’t look like a priest.

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
Make me One with everything,
a drop returning to the ocean,
But not yet- because the passion in me is still
becoming unique and we never talked about that,
only the fear and courage to separate from the Cloud
and fall, thinking I will be alone when I am so fragile
that light splits through me, prismatic.

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
I’ll have what she’s having,
holy children. Do you hear the Mother laughing?
She will take you on a tour of heaven,
wherein you love them with your eyes and they try
to put your whole chin in their raspberry mouths.

A Buddhist, a Jew and a priest walk into a bar.
Darlin’ you’re lookin’ at that glass wall
Like a cow looks at a new gate-
Or sometimes it’s not glass at all.

The bar sometimes seems to be helping
(pray for peace, and rule of law,
for without it people might eat each other alive)
-and sometimes the law inside turn us out.
