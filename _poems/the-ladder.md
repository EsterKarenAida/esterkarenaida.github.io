One stands on the shoulders of another.
So the falls crack louder, higher footing
gained for the wave's crest, the life of my mother.

The Ba’al Shem once prayed on and on. The others
drifted out – chipped his ladder, shook his footing.
One is lifted on the shoulders of another,

he taught. Will you scramble to your lover
clutching skeins of her hair? Reach by putting
word upon word, yours upon mine. No mother

waits so expectant as She who made you. A knowing
shivered through them, prayer a bell cutting
through the clouds. Though each stood on the shoulders

of another, they did not falter,
unlike my father, whose death declared his doubt in
life. At forty I take up the life of my father,

continue it, for having lived longer
than he.  I no longer know which way I’m looking,
nor which stands on the shoulders of the other,
but bend to your lives as I am rung,  mother.
