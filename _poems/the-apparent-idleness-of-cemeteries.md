title: The Apparent Idleness of Cemeteries

drafted: August 2018
manuscript: Centrifuge Rattle

  From the poems she had finished but not yet published. Her notes cite

I choose a world in which crimson sheets
simply signify a girls’ dorm,
a buzzing section of honeycomb.

Just out of sight of the thick slabs,
doves mewl to their young. In the petting zoo
the guinea pig huddles into the rabbit’s rib,
making fools of the living who know
who they are with dead certainty.

On Idleness. I wanted most then, to wander
like a river: last thoughts form and deliquesce
like clouds watched long enough,
like a stream in its gathering-against
jasper or sandstone.

We have forgotten the skill of societies,
as the inosculation of trees through
chain-link and cement, to make small adjustments;
as in driving, when sighting an obstacle.

I choose the living hive.
Yet — what is this mutation?
My nation of soldiers, will we recognize ourselves
when they put us to work like the German army
of today, pouring concrete pads for refugees,
an emergency clinic on the road to Beitar?

Is this the right question?

…and of all the sowing…
and the reaping, have mercy and love
issued forth.
Mercy, white milk, and honey, gold love…

— Shirts with laces for want of buttons.
Some of us planted citron and orange,
some, a line of Sabras.

Enter the bee-keeper. And wildflowers in the copse,
startling hues of butterflies.

Admor miRozhyn, recall to me once more
how these heads of wheat knocking against one another in the fields
will at length give forth the integrity of bread?

Tell me again that when the Angel Michael
initiates, Gabriel too is moved to lend a hand?

Is there still a market for honey?

Is this the right question?

Do words breathe between us, move us,
are we still enough to notice? Is our Shabbat
a sunset field dialed down dim enough, and wide a mesh, and idle,
to catch observations native to us?

I suppose it’s possible to glimpse the marine-blue curtain
of a defunct construction site, flapping slack at a distance,
and imagine life where the only touch is air;

or conjure winged monarchs, black-segmented flames flying,
precursors to a colored hymn of stained glass.

Unlike the Dream of Akinosuke, watched over by his friends,
I cannot know whose mouth it is, from which this butterfly
emerges, any more than I know
whose mouth spoke more than one species of redemption
nor all the names for the unknown.
