When I stood before a podium to
graduate, it was two months since I stood
on the chair to loosen my stepfather.
The mind would like to be a net. It would
feed us, so that no one would die. I don’t
recall what I said, or how he received
me. I think instead of what drew my father
to Ithaca, scene of shards numerous
as the colored maelstrom of your Venice
glass-shop as it swirled with tinkling music.
On my balcony still is the toy Hess
truck he found at a convention at a
sterile Howard Johnson’s, a gift to my
brother when she collected me again.
