The wind is rattling at the glass in Jerusalem;
I wondered if the power might go out in the hail.
In the dark of night I put a sleeping bag
over my son. So cold.

Parshat Bo: the rabbanut taught us, we cannot join
the table of freedom
when the impurity of the dead is upon us. But when
one family joins another (each alone being too small)
there is no need for blood on the doorpost
of an empty house. No blood.

Do not read “rabbanut” but rather, “Ribbonut.”

If I could speak to you with the intimacy of poems
I would tell you that God answers us everywhere,
if only we have hearts to listen.  Once a pigeon,
dalmation-spotted and beautiful, accompanied me
the length of a city-block on foot because I
could not fly. On friday night my youngest daughter
dramatized the Book of Samuel for forty minutes
while the others were buried in their books. You begin
with Awe of God.

I don’t believe revelation is a stick to whack with-
it is not ours if it does not arise from within us-
so I don’t understand why Moses blurts out all God’s
demands up front. Not just men but women.
Not just people but animals. The firstborn- so responsible
for their society- and the advisers are khopping this
but Pharoah is hung up on the goats. A heart can be
A hailstone.

Bags of warm clothing from the community
are cluttering my bedroom. (Imi you are so American!)
-Please- help me with this mess in my house?
How much choice do you have- can you refuse a gift?
You know us- we get ridiculously insulted.

And the young soldier you meet may breathe
A sigh of relief: who but a Jewish mama is going to
Weigh you down with way too many layers; bags upon bags
Of baby clothes for the neighbors.

So let’s just negotiate, old friends, about the number of bags.
It will rain. Let’s say, two for each one who comes out to us?
