When I think of my teen flight to the house
of grandparents I never knew, over a sea,
love and spoiling and my dutiful willingness
for a discipline I chose almost contractually-

I see that now I’ve begun. I enact a return
 to yet tighter roots in the second part of life.

Lining the drive, the patches of conifers are taller
now; the stray lilac and birch seem more lovely to me.
The same piles of magazines on the carpet,
shelves chock with gilt china behind closed doors

don’t make my fists curl, they even reassure a little.

I want to find the place in my many-drawered soul
for the Christian air I was raised on. To pay tribute
to family like to adoptive, and golden teachers. I turn
weathervane, wind directing me. I cup a hand and

stoop to sip,  from a whole nation that has held
its blessings for me intact for thirty years,
a hope chest lined with cedar.
