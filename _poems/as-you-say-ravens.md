if you wd have me speak
you must learn the tongue of my dead & loved ones

Your wings, more lead than steel, protest. Each
downstroke is ease to your ache. Yet moment by
moment repeats and demands: your resting
lids bring the body, in their wake, to rest;

now a lift of your eyes requires
a massive surge to your shoulders
but maintains your body in thin air, travelling,
travelling. A vantage native to you.

Does your body argue with itself,
        one wing deaf to another, imbalanced
or dense with symmetry? The currents bear
         their fingers on each, testing which is

stronger: Left- What is worth this endless search,
        Right: Let me be a speck of coal, mimic
the inert ground.

        *

What, then? A pocked body
ancient as your trek. Olive tree bearing fruit, lodging.
        And inside: nostrils awake to cypress;
a nest, cupped palms, a drink of water.
        Spongy as overgrown grass. Kids rush in

to somersault on a king koil.
        A day’s encounter in motion, sky-
loving-- at day’s end, rest. A woman’s hands
could never make your bed in a room; her

eyes lift to sparks of gold-shot evergreen,
azure.  Dense cover, Heaven frames you.
           love is the name we give to our pursuit of wholeness.

*

Your heart’s an Anasazi shelf, close
        kernels;  dark characters and thoughts
they house, at one.  Endless forest and Whole
Foods Cosmos, You are one with your Source as

a shell with its tortoise.  God has shot an
arrow of our history; I search
for it across a sweeping arc, shape of
our mandala, two tribes woven, sonoric.

 I  print images of blue-eyed grass, their pupils, each a starry bulb of light;
            and  japanese water irises, indigo - needing to drink in that color.
            Family iridaceae, brushed and sprayed with water.
I only make myself miss your work.
Compassion is the name we give to our pursuit of wholeness.

Just look at this cedar, encompassing
two black thieves, their broke branch-craft.  And do I
say so much caw as the light dims rose? As
you say: the ravens are coming to roost.
Rest is the name we give to our sojourn
in wholeness.
