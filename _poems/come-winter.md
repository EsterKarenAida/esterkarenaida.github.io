---
title: Come Winter
subtitle: צוהר תעשה לתיבה — for our parents
opening: Who planted this tree? Who tends it still?
drafted: May 2020
note: From the poems she had finished but not yet published.
---

Who planted this tree? Who tends it still?
Tree, be, in this present,
door to life and family?
Branches gnarled, pleasing
as a bonsai.
Falling petals, each one
sunrise and moon.
To sit an hour or two beneath such shelter.
To learn from a tree to be
anchored as a boulder, or squat to the ground,
equanimous. Above, gently swaying
no matter the antics of this world,
its dizzying speed.
To be enfolded in its lace canopy
backlit by dappled light as the clouds
shift. This petal. That petal. Falling on my lap. To sip
an infusion of lavender and lemon verbena.

Let its shade and its life-giving breath praise it.
Come winter, bald as the moon, and beautiful.
It says, come in, come home, your innermost core
is where the sap flows. Find in the frame of my limbs
an aperture: light connects you to
a vast universe.
It sways a little and says, come out: the oak
and the elderberry are even now offering sustenance
one to another, at the roots, like the citron and the palm.
