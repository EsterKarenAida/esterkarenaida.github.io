A day of rest, and a faded color print of Monet’s Nympheas,
hanging in the bathroom, leaped into the arms of my dulled mind.

I entered a dream or a life of a man possessed by love;
I beheld his beloved in a thousand poses, and witnessed him

before the mirror on his knees, an eye of that mirror projecting
light; two dark fishes cavorted all within a bright, wing-splayed

swallow at the base of his skull. His heart’s visions calligraphed
in the water, days of couplehood, children who must be carried.

I was brought to think, as they lay in repose or embrace, that
it’s the darkest hours that bring forth grace, though I wonder.

Is it a dim quiet that allows me to see the regard these two
bring to days together? Swift strokes, as if formed of light.

The deer, the owl, the ancient mask: all civilization in dream-
scape comes to rest in them, as free as a boy’s mind

sketching in the margins of a dull lesson.

And today I read your essay; I fall back a little from my own
myriad thoughts, but the place I fall back to

is a precise map for you. For me it is a sooty, rich dark in which
I rest; that I listen to; my pond is sustained by the waters of

a great lake, enriched by your exuberant vision that loves
the hills like lambs. I hope we’ll sing Hallel when we can.
