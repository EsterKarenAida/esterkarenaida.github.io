We dozed off in class, Shabbat Hanukka,
but even so Israel recalls, Shammai says begin with eight candles,
decrease by one each night. Like the number of sacrifices
brought by the nations to Jerusalem over Sukkot.
After joining us, the guests left one by one,
leaving us alone.

--No, Hillel says, always increase the light.

‘Shanghai makes Chicago look like a backwater.
The Chinese implicitly understand each other
in a way I think I have never experienced before.’

‘Miracle Election in Alabama’

‘Palestinian with suicide belt stabs guard, is shot’

What words are there for this?

I write most days to stave dark silence

after the honk of geese lifts off from Lake Cayuga
now icing at the edges under a fragile snow lace
knowing my father has died

Til the Book of Maccabees, the boys from Skokie
never heard much history: with the house a wreck
we made Sukkot late, among all the nations like myrtle switches,
willows, palm boughs and citron all wrapped up in one.
The dedication, as usual, eight days.

The kids came in from Tel Aviv, Even Shmuel, Gedera,
Jerusalem College of Tech, Talpiot.
I bite my lip about one moving to Netanya-
as I did about South Dakota and Germany,
for if one essential human need is for connection,
another is surely for autonomy.

I wanted to make a refuge for all wild.
My couches look more like a petting-zoo now, adolescents
and love-birds sprawled or perched on every surface.

Your message surprises by moving me to tears
for reasons I myself am unsure of-
vicarious wonder mixed with longing mixed

What words?

I am mumbling through grace after meals when it hits me,
the foundation that allows for ingathering-
we were released -again- from the rule oppressing us.
Once independent,  we set about rebuilding our house.
I beg myself,
what am I that oppresses the soul who will awaken tomorrow?

In a scene from La Belle Verte, the wife returns late
to find her bed, all the beds, the balcony-
taken over by sleepers, think of the breathing,
the slew of sneakers on the welcome mat.

She eases her way into the sofa,
tossing one arm over her husband (nowhere else to put it.)
Her muscles relaxing, she murmurs,
Is the whole world in our house?
