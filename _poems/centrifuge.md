**I**

Post-transplant my blood raged–the fourteen-day landmark as with Shimon and Darwish

Zehava?  you've overshot us      I  see you in a dress that repeats the rust lines of your hair

My cot, match to yours.   On it, my bead-box in disarray           the turquoise I strung for you trails to an end

Half-light of your love's  footfalls past my bed before work         helmet and haiku in hand

Your cells of days rock in their drum
Like his murmur against an encroaching seafoam wall, five or seven splash out

**II**

my blood betrays blood

Plasmapheresis: body rendered fluid in the turning drum

I slouched in an armchair poor folks what are they sick with

good try not to move your arm   brain in a three-hour bath  I am so still I breed mosquitoes but it's antibodies skimmed from the jetting surface of my blood

 it wasn't painful.  left a quirky moment when I rose to the john and crumpled

everything will be caught in a seven-year centrifuge

**III**

it's 2 AM, and only Darwish is aware of my unrest

we never know which one of us will be called first    how Darwish will trade two years of life for a heart                how four years on infection stings him

the kids named it Ziggy, the whirring electric pump twinned to my heart.
is it as easy to take out as it was to implant it?
this so absorbs me, the night there is a true heart, I have no  worry left for the sewing of the new heart into my body

The lobby is dotted with prayer-grottoes of  paper hot cups, café turki crusting in their wells. I cannot sit so I pace

I call my surgeon.  2:35 am, his Hebrew cadence reassures from my Nokia: I won't need caffeine...it will be, the gravity of this thing— I'll be wired

Led by the curve of his bloated liver, Darwish eases out of his cot

Blood flashes through the LVAD at 8,000 RPM, fueled by two batteries holstered at my hips Clint Eastwood-style

in a  flash at Kfar Qassem, the machines reversed

Darwish and I shuffle past the after-hours doors of Hadassa's  psych clinic

At the end of a shadowy hall we reach the soft neon, the pretty  fish.

**IV**

Let me speak of my aquarium.

What will it be tonight      there now it's the dinner cart jangling toward your curtained enclosure            but who chooses             your hand of cards tossed to the burning drum
you hold each baby in your heart’s focus, hold fast as fringe, fast as her chubby grip on your shirt hem, close to your body as a misbaha rosary

caught up in the Shabbat mezze of my prior life I fumbled at the stroller's spring, I poured down Rte 1 to Bar Ilan  where’s the papaya stick the baby gnaws, I panted, entering lovely classes

I could not fathom telling my self–to-be: say only what, within,  you are forced to

which of us will be called like expectant fathers
or is this the curfew that drowns suns

Kfar Qassem is not on my map                I was not afraid, then I was afraid

**V**

Underfoot, what will be left now

I lower my chin, take in calloused heels, that same crack in the tiled floor.
the room i always wake in

All-Merciful my bail is anything You can  lift    take speech my spine the bones of dreams             batteries dead in the charging drum

flick drops of blood for the doorpost       take blanched T-cells         take teeth

when I stand I slump         when we say 'shortness of breath' -it's shortness of blood, we mean     chalk-bone, niche

**VI**

I make picture after picture of my burning house     gutted as a fish

Once, we converted from wood-stove to coal-burning
I begin to know myself in a thin black ash that settles on everything                    is nowhere

   I back out of each prayer let my soul be unto others as dust
   if you flip channels your interstices entertain dreams of others

 Through  the glass of a bus I half-eye a flat fabric  where, spinning, earth deadheaded the roses. The motions of a man in a cloth hat, an ant,

his mind a village barred to me             what am I holding                no keys

the blood we know, familiar—until we remember that the body replaces itself

going up in the charring drum

 by order of my autoimmune syndrome: my blood storms its own.
no localized organ it's Tel Aviv's clammy envelope

my black ash reaches my milk.

**VII**

Later, the humidity of Cyclosporine will wilt my bit of fire in a drum
I lodge in gaps between oncoming thought-cargoes

blood counts read like speed limits     my throat is thick:
there is a father, and I wish I knew how many children

we put it to him, this hit-and-run of the mind:          your wife is brain-dead

her heart on life support still beats          will you let us take it

another woman may live              to my own heart it sounds too intimate to be borne

how can one single scale serve up that blow and cast rebirth

I warn myself: have care for causation
but the pivot (it clangs like the bell to which we,  dense world, are tongue)            lies in will

and absence of will I'm thanking God that her death is now beyond human help

and hang my thoughts how can I have said this there can be no thanks for a young mother's death but God?

bless and hold Isa          who begrudged me nothing

while blood runs             after blood

while I know nothing of him I can speculate in magicked terror or
bless the husband who summoned will

to turn from his unbegun grief.

 In a culture which covers mirrors
toss a sheet over the flat world

**VII**

on Rte. 1 a small organ whistles by in a large ambulance

chatty medics   which of us will be called     who chooses

in shuffles Isa gutted            and children full to drown with ash

In one vision he looks up         and though I am foreign to myself
we are familiar to each other

it's here now his  act is kindled   in that charging drum where ash speaks to ash

He offers my children the attention, tender, tenacious,   his own have known.

My future heart will search, testify, rehearse this act of your heart of flesh in me Isa
