Walk with me out past the dust-bin, where
“The gate of tears is never locked” is plastered.

When he will be ten or twelve
the only ribbon of yellow will be
in time-lapse photography, a stream
of headlights on a decent highway through Dura.

He’ll choose to tell the old stories if he likes,
or lead tours of the Dajani Aqueduct,
one  paved arm at Yad VaShem, one arm to Vizhnitz,
both pointing to the astonished gap, then
silent scream of memory that is Deir Yassin.

Those arms embrace even now, as once
a Holocaust survivor and a young German
found themselves holding one another
in the throat of the tomb before they understood it.

So many answers to our questions
poke us from some new direction --
torn up by my father’s death, in the same day
I heard of a child coming into the world;

surely children are a way God comforts,
and teaches us what He means, at once.
Who among us has not had to forgive a child?

Your giving hearts stand stout
in enduring terraces that hold up my landscape.
It’s after a rain. Now, even granite
might recall a wish to be this new perennial--
so soft to touch--  lambs’ ears in December.
